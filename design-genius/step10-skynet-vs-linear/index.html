The three largest gaps

01

Headlines are barely tracked

Our display type sits at -0.005em. Linear holds -0.022em at every size above 32px, without exception. That is a gap of four and a half times, and it is the single reason our headlines read soft next to theirs.

02

The accent is doing too many jobs

Amber appears six times in the first screen and is the most common text colour on the whole page, 39 uses. Linear shows zero saturated colour above the fold. Their accent works because it is rationed.

03

Borders instead of an elevation ladder

We draw a solid 1px ivory border 19 times and ship no shadows at all. Linear separates surfaces with 5 percent hairlines and inset shadows, climbing four named levels from canvas to tint.

03 · Elevation

Shadow treatment

Ours · no shadows measured

solid 1px ivory border, used 19 times

A fully opaque light border is the loudest way to draw an edge. It reads as a box around content rather than a change of surface.

Linear · 8 shadows, 3 inset

inset 0 0 0 1px #23252a

The edge sits inside the card and shares the family of the canvas. Elevation comes from the surface step, not from the outline.

04 · Accent

Accent frequency

Ours · first screen

6

Six saturated objects before a single scroll: kicker, logo, nav button, hero italic, two buttons. Across the page amber is used 39 times as text, more than any neutral.

Linear · first screen

0

Not one saturated colour above the fold. Their acid lime exists, but it is held back for a single filled action further down. Restraint is what gives it force when it finally appears.

Keep the colour. It is a strong, ownable amber and it is ours. The change is how often it is allowed to speak, not what it is.

05 · Radii

Border radius vocabulary

Six distinct radii shipped, all within ten pixels of one another. The differences are invisible; the inconsistency is not.

8px 10px 12px 14px 18px 24px

Linear ships five and they are far apart on purpose: 2px, 4px, 6px, 12px, pill. Cards are always 12. Collapse ours to 6 for controls, 12 for cards, pill for chips, and the vocabulary becomes readable.

07 · Judgment

Two changes that need a call from you

Needs a decision

The serif is carrying the brand alone

Fraunces at weight 300 is the only thing separating this from a generic dark product page, and it is also the reason tracking looks soft: a light serif at 92px needs tighter tracking than a grotesque, not looser. Either commit to the serif and tighten it hard, or move display type to the grotesque and keep the serif for one pull quote. Both are defensible. Doing neither is what reads as unfinished.

Needs a decision

Amber is both the brand and the interface

The same amber marks the logo, every section kicker, every link and both buttons. Rationing it to one filled action per screen will make the page calmer and the buttons louder, but it also makes the page less obviously ours at a glance. The call is whether brand recognition or hierarchy matters more on the first screen.

08 · Patch

Values to swap in your token file

:root {
  /* surfaces: four named levels, opaque, no washes */
  --surface-0: #0b0c0e;   /* canvas, unchanged        */
  --surface-1: #131518;   /* cards, was rgba(255,255,255,.03) */
  --surface-2: #1a1d21;   /* elevated, was .04        */
  --surface-3: #23252a;   /* tint and edges           */

  /* edges: hairline, not a solid outline */
  --hairline:  rgba(242, 239, 232, 0.06);  /* was #f2efe8 solid */
  --edge-card: inset 0 0 0 1px rgba(242, 239, 232, 0.06);

  /* type: tracking is the single highest leverage change */
  --track-display: -0.022em;  /* was -0.005em */
  --track-body:    -0.011em;  /* was normal   */

  /* radii: three values, far apart */
  --r-control: 6px;
  --r-card:    12px;
  --r-pill:    9999px;

  /* rhythm */
  --container:  1280px;   /* was 1180px */
  --section-y:  128px;    /* was 104px  */
}
